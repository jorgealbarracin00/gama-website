## 1. The project that started the journey

CASH CAST was the creator’s first **serious GAMA DYNAMICS software project**. It began with a personal need rather than a commercial product plan:

> “When am I going to run out of money?”

The second question made it a forecasting project: could that moment become visible early enough to do something about it?

A bank balance records a current position. Rent, utilities, everyday spending allowances, card obligations and a salary that arrives later can produce a different future. CASH CAST sought to make that future understandable without requiring the person to become an accountant.

The creator recalls approximately twelve major iterations. This is creator testimony, not a claim that twelve tagged versions, releases or complete screenshot sets survive. The inspected earlier FinanceApp repository retains two commits; the later KASU/CashCast repository retains 62. The exhibition presents four source-backed design directions, with honest visual reconstructions.

As the model and interface grew, complexity exceeded what the creator could confidently maintain and simplify at that stage. Development paused. Subsequent GAMA DYNAMICS work has strengthened the engineering experience that began here. Modern development tools, including Codex, may make difficult work easier to revisit; they do not establish a return date.

**Paused. Not forgotten.**

## 2. The manifesto

**Financial clarity without financial judgment.**

CASH CAST is a navigator. When a route changes, it recalculates. It does not punish the person for changing direction.

The five principles:

1. **No financial homework.** Focus on meaningful financial events, expected income, obligations, goals and configurable living allowances. Do not demand that every coffee be recorded.
2. **Forecasting instead of compulsory budgeting.** Explain the potential consequences of the current path. Budgets must not become a compulsory admission requirement.
3. **No guilt.** No spending grades, broken streaks, shame messages or artificial financial morality. A changed circumstance changes the forecast.
4. **Hide the machinery.** Present an understandable answer first. Charts, tables and technical explanations belong in optional detail when they help.
5. **The user remains in control.** Inform, forecast and compare alternatives. Do not dictate the decision.

The central statement is:

> YOUR MONEY. YOUR DECISIONS. YOUR FUTURE.

The native help text independently supports this philosophy: future-first planning, reduced mental load, no compulsory category budgets, no shame dashboards and no pressure to log every coffee. This establishes intent; it does not imply that every unfinished historical interface achieved the ideal.

The website must never celebrate borrowing, assign a moral value to spending, or shame a negative forecast. Its response to a shortfall is a clear explanation and an invitation to explore another assumption.

## 3. Forecasting philosophy

The financial weather forecast is the governing metaphor. Rain is information. The person may take an umbrella, change a plan or continue as intended. A possible negative cash balance deserves the same calm treatment.

The useful questions are:

- Will the money last until the next income?
- Which commitment changes the picture?
- When is the lowest projected point?
- What happens if income arrives later?
- What changes if a payment moves by agreement?
- When could a goal fit while preserving other commitments?

A forecast is an estimate derived from available events and explicit assumptions. It is not a guarantee of actual bank settlement, affordability outside its horizon, lender eligibility or personal financial success.

The browser presentation uses one financial horizon rather than a wall of dashboards. The main answers are Today, Coming Up, Lowest Point and Attention. The event table exists to make the answer inspectable, not to impose transaction chores.

## 4. Engineering philosophy: the beast and the controller

The creator’s metaphor is:

> “A nuclear power plant operated with a PS5 controller.”

It describes the ambition to place a sophisticated engine beneath a familiar, approachable interface. It does not describe a physical nuclear system, game-controller integration or an association with Sony or PlayStation.

**Complexity is our problem. Simplicity is the user’s right.**

The cinematic engine represents interconnected income, commitments, cash truth, goals, payment timing and credit experiments. The conceptual dependency map lets a visitor inspect these ideas without pretending to be a live trace of native calculations.

The controller moment brings that machinery back to one question, one approaching event, a short explanation and a few useful choices. The interactive phone is a website concept using the browser model. It is not a native screenshot or proof that a next-generation CashCast interface has shipped.

### Financial validation limits

The card model stores a limit, balance, active flag, policy and billing day. It does not provide a complete lender model: APR, fees, separate statement closing date and payment due date, grace-period eligibility, minimum payments, cash advances and promotional conditions are absent.

A native repayment-capacity function sums positive projected cash stocks across successive weeks. Carried cash may therefore be counted repeatedly. That calculation requires review before any safe-borrow claim.

Native projections group events by week. Income later in a week can offset an earlier bill in the weekly total and hide an intraweek shortfall. Contract comments and implementation also diverge in places.

The retained primary native test file contains only an empty generated example. Debug invariant assertions are useful but are not a comprehensive financial test suite. The website must not call the native engine financially certified, lender-complete or production-ready.

## 9. The six chapters

### 01 — The question

**“When will my money run out?”**

Begin with $2,000. Let the visitor watch scheduled events change the balance and reveal a calculated −$350 before payday. Then ask: what if you saw it coming?

The fictional schedule, derived shortfall, horizon and optional event table make this an explanation rather than an unrelated animation. The fox moves the emotional tone toward clarity.

### 02 — The rebellion

**“Your money is not your homework.”**

Present the five principles with a consistent custom vector icon language, the authentic fox and the creator’s refusal to track every coffee. The contrast is between recording every detail and understanding meaningful future events, without claiming that all other finance applications are wrong.

### 03 — Borrowing from tomorrow

**“Could tomorrow help you reach your goals today?”**

Show two equally considered futures. WAIT searches for a cash purchase date that retains the stated buffer through the remaining horizon. ACCELERATE introduces an earlier qualifying purchase and an explicit full repayment obligation under fictional terms.

Visitors can alter the goal cost, test eligibility assumptions and stress both plans with late income plus an unexpected cost. Earlier access is a trade-off, not a recommendation.

### 04 — The beast

**“Every simple answer hides a thousand calculations.”**

Reveal the cinematic forecasting engine. The interactive architecture map explains cash truth, events, goals, credit cycles, forecasts and a clearer answer. Each explanation carries the appropriate source/experimental/visionary status.

More source code is not the same as a validated financial model. Known gaps are part of the narrative.

### 05 — The controller

**“Power underneath. Simplicity on the surface.”**

Bring the engine back to a simple phone concept. A visitor moves one agreed mock payment and sees the calculated answer change.

The evolution gallery presents four retained design directions; current-source native captures provide a separate evidence layer. Reconstructions and authentic captures must never share an ambiguous label.

### 06 — The unfinished beginning

**“The project that started everything.”**

Tell the human story of ambition, learning and the decision to pause. Finish with the fox overlooking a luminous horizon.

## 10. Browser forecasting contract

The website model is `lib/cashcast/model.ts`. It is independent of the paused native engine and intentionally simpler.

Money uses integer AUD cents. Dates are fictional UTC calendar days. Every event has a stable ID, a date, a positive amount, an income/expense direction and a description. Sorting is deterministic: date first, income before expenses on the same day, then stable event ID. Actual bank settlement may differ from that same-day assumption.

The calculation applies every event to a running balance. The first negative point and lowest point are derived from that sequence, including intermediate event balances. Daily closing values drive the horizon curve; all event-level balances remain inspectable. A negative modelled payment is an unfunded obligation, not evidence that a bank executed it.

The default horizon runs from **4 May to 8 June 2030**, 35 elapsed days with 36 inclusive daily points.

| Date | Stable ID | Mock event | Change | Balance after |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 4 May | Opening | Available cash | — | $2,000 |
| 8 May | rent-01 | Rent | −$1,200 | $800 |
| 10 May | living-01 | Living allowance | −$200 | $600 |
| 12 May | utilities-01 | Utilities | −$180 | $420 |
| 15 May | card-01 | Existing card statement | −$770 | **−$350** |
| 18 May | salary-01 | Salary | +$3,200 | $2,850 |
| 21 May | living-02 | Living allowance | −$220 | $2,630 |
| 24 May | rent-02 | Next rent | −$1,200 | $1,430 |
| 27 May | utilities-02 | Next utilities | −$180 | $1,250 |
| 31 May | living-03 | Living allowance | −$220 | $1,030 |
| 1 June | salary-02 | Next salary | +$3,200 | $4,230 |
| 4 June | card-02 | Another card payment | −$350 | $3,880 |
| 7 June | living-04 | Living allowance | −$220 | $3,660 |

The default first shortfall and lowest point are both **−$350 on 15 May**, after the existing card statement. The closing balance is $3,660. All carry-forward days retain the last calculated balance.

Controls:

- **Move a payment:** only rent-01 or utilities-01 within their fictional agreed, fee-free windows. Card deadlines remain fixed. A real change requires the payee’s agreement.
- **Add income:** insert a hypothetical income from presets; no personal information required.
- **Unexpected expense:** insert an illustrative surprise expense.
- **Explore a goal:** add an explicit cash-purchase event. This is separate from the card comparison.
- **Compare futures:** compare calculated original and modified minima, first shortfall and closing balance.
- **Reset:** restore the original schedule, cursor and comparison state.

Moving the first rent to **19 May** preserves its ID and amount, removes the early shortfall, raises the minimum to $850 and leaves the $3,660 closing balance unchanged. This demonstrates why timing can matter independently of the total.

The model rejects invalid cents, duplicate IDs, invalid event dates and arithmetic overflow. Added events receive stable unique IDs. No authentication, bank connection, financial credentials or real financial input is requested.

### Exclusions and uncertainty

No overdraft charges, interest, tax, inflation, investment return, banking settlement latency or probabilistic confidence interval is calculated. Living allowances stand in for everyday spending. A finite horizon cannot establish long-term affordability. Unknown future events remain unknown.

“No shortfall in this example” means exactly that. It must not become “you are safe” or “you can afford this.”

## 11. Goal and card experiment

The dedicated comparison begins with rent already moved by agreement to 19 May, so the earlier shortfall does not obscure the goal decision. Both paths retain all other commitments.

The default goal costs $2,000, with a $300 cash buffer.

### PATH A — WAIT

Search each possible purchase date for the earliest cash purchase that keeps at least $300 **after the purchase and through the remaining forecast**. This is a finite-horizon check, not an instruction to save or a lifelong affordability guarantee.

Default outcome: purchase **1 June 2030**, lowest cash across the whole horizon $850, closing cash $1,660.

### PATH B — ACCELERATE

Fictional card terms:

- Qualifying retail purchase on **6 May 2030**.
- Statement closes **25 May 2030**.
- Full purchase plus the stated **$10 fee** due **6 June 2030**.
- $2,500 available credit after existing balances; the purchase plus fee must fit.
- Prior statements must be paid in full.
- The $770 statement due 15 May belongs to this same hypothetical card and remains an eligibility-linked obligation.
- The $350 payment due 4 June belongs to another card. It affects cash coverage but does not itself determine this card’s contractual interest-free conditions.

The purchase reserves credit; it does not create free cash. The model inserts the full $2,010 repayment at the explicit deadline. It never substitutes a minimum payment. If eligibility assumptions fail, the arrangement is blocked and no borrowing is inserted.

Default outcome: purchase **6 May**, full repayment funded under the stated assumptions, $300 buffer retained, lowest cash $850 and closing cash $1,650. The $10 difference from WAIT is the explicitly modelled fee.

This is a hypothetical example, not a universal statement about interest-free cards. Real provider terms control dates, fees, interest and eligibility. A forecast ending before the repayment date cannot establish repayment coverage.

### Stress the original plans

The stress control delays the second salary from 1 June to **8 June** and adds an **$800 expense on 3 June**. Original purchase/repayment plans remain fixed for comparison.

Default stressed outcomes:

- WAIT: minimum −$2,340; closing $860; original buffer not retained. A newly recalculated cash-purchase date becomes 8 June, shown separately from the original 1 June plan.
- ACCELERATE: minimum −$2,350; closing $850; full repayment on 6 June is not funded and the stated interest-free conditions fail.

The model does not invent penalty interest or default charges when conditions fail. It explains that actual consequences depend on the provider.

Eligibility, full repayment coverage, linked prior-statement coverage, all-cash commitment coverage and buffer preservation are distinct checks. A contractually qualifying purchase can still be a poor fit for a person’s cash commitments. Neither path is automatically preferred.

The visual language is near-black, soft white, magenta and cyan. Amber identifies a potential shortfall through text as well as colour. Cinematic scenes, precise editorial typography and optional machinery details support the product’s calm, human purpose.

Desktop and tablet can show parallel paths. Mobile stacks them, uses touch-friendly presets and a day slider, and keeps the primary answer readable. The mobile horizon is supported by textual answers and an accessible event table rather than relying on a shrunken chart.

Accessibility requirements include semantic headings, labelled native controls, keyboard operation, visible focus, pressed-state buttons, descriptive image alternatives, live status messages and textual warning states. Decorative vectors are hidden from assistive technology. Reduced-motion CSS removes decorative animation, transitions and smooth scrolling. The forecast also provides a manual day control; playback is user initiated and pauses advancement while the document is hidden.

Raster artwork is WebP with responsive Next/Image sizing. Above-the-fold art can be prioritized; later artwork and captures should load lazily. No new heavy financial/charting dependency is needed. CSS and SVG render the interface; animation never drives the accounting.
